# Chapter 34: Religion as Education

<a id="p1"></a>I am going to treat the subject of religion with a particular emphasis-as an arena of education rather than as a system of belief or a set of ideas or hopeful expectations. As education, religion requires the introduction and application of new information to real life situations and the adoption of certain practices for personal development. In education you also maintain goals for your own development and for their possible use in the future. When people think of religion, they often don't think of education. They think of adopting, defending or struggling with belief. We don't wish to treat religion in this manner. Simply said, religion is practice. It is what you use to develop and improve yourself and, from this, the world around you.

<a id="p2"></a>Therefore, we can leave conjecture and arguments behind and look at what can be done for you and what you can do for others. Our Teaching is religious in nature because it deals with applying a greater truth to the world. What is a greater truth? A greater truth is something people do not make up for themselves for their personal reassurance, comfort or for any other reason. It is something that is inherent within people. They either choose to acknowledge it or not, to use it or not. People experience and interpret it individually, but the truth is beyond interpretation. What people think of as their experience of the truth is their interpretation of the truth. The truth always beckons you beyond your ideals and your philosophy. As a great attraction beyond your own grasp, it keeps engaging you to move forward. So, when people say, "I know the truth here" and they argue with others, they are arguing over interpretations and approaches. That is because the truth appears to be different things at different stages of development. It has different value and application. When someone at this stage argues with someone at that stage, they do not understand one another because their experiences are determined by their stages of development as well as their own personal preferences, fears and psychological make-up.

<a id="p3"></a>Why is the truth important for people to know? Because it is a source of purpose, meaning and direction in life. Purpose, meaning and direction are what all human pursuit is for. Even in the worst error or the most foolish behavior, someone is trying to experience purpose, meaning and direction. Beyond fundamental survival, this is the human quest: to know why you are here, what you serve, where you must go and what you must do. Purpose, meaning and direction. This is what religion is for. I am going to talk about religion in terms of practice and not in terms of theological ideas or God. It does not matter if you think God is real or not. That just determines your form of education and what terminology must be selected for you to proceed.

<a id="p4"></a>You are all religious people because you are involved in education. School is not out. It is moving forward. When you cease to learn, your mind begins to shrink in. It is like a muscle in your body. It must be exercised and employed and given new challenges. That is why the goal of life is not to achieve complete understanding. The goal of life here is to achieve the ability to be educated and to share education with others. That means you have come very far and are at the beginning all at once because the truth is still beyond you. What does this yield to you? It yields purpose, meaning and direction-something the world itself cannot provide. This is the source of your inspiration, enthusiasm and experience of life.

<a id="p5"></a>So, first of all: God is real. Everywhere, real. So what? What good does that do you today and tomorrow? The important contribution of religion is the development of the human mind and the advancement of the human spirit. Growth involves friction, confrontation, success, failure, everything. When people lose vigor for life and enthusiasm, it means their ability to learn in this environment has diminished or is complete. If it is complete, then it is time for them to leave. If they can do so in a conscious manner, it means they can leave with very little regret, and that is good.

<a id="p6"></a>Most people are not in this position. They have lost vigor because they do not see their life as education. They do not see religion as practice. Dilemmas, within yourself and externally as well, are not the issue. Problems are healthy for you but can only be valued if you are involved in your development. Development means you are assuming a curriculum you did not invent for yourself. If you invent a curriculum for yourself, you will merely entertain yourself with your past learning. That is not advancement. It takes great trust to engage in a curriculum you did not create for yourself. People say, "I do not want to learn this. It affronts my ideas. I do not believe in this, so I do not wish to study it or investigate it." That is not progressing.

<a id="p7"></a>We had considered talking about gratitude, but to do that we would need to talk about education, for it is the only thing you can be truly grateful for. Everything you see in this room, everything you see out the window, people have worked to create to give you opportunity, but the opportunity is not for comfort alone. It is for advancement. Everything you see, be it agreeable or disagreeable, is for advancement, but if you do not see advancement as your fundamental aim, then you will judge something merely on whether it pleases you or not or provides comfort or not.

<a id="p8"></a>In our school here, we teach the reclamation of Knowledge, the greatest and most fundamental use of the mind. As this becomes developed in the individual, it provides a sound demonstration of the mind's true capabilities and the assistance that is necessary both from certain individuals in the world and from forces beyond the world. This solves the question about God but not in an ultimate sense. It just means that you can experience a Presence that you cannot account for through your vision and which you must in time acknowledge as a necessary part of your advancement.

<a id="p9"></a>Therefore, I have said education is for advancement and religion is for education. Now, let me make another important point so we all can understand: Your advancement is not for you. This must be understood. Advancement is not for you. Your advancement is your contribution because from it will come all manner of actions, words and demonstrations that are necessary. The advancing person will use both the greatest inspiration as well as the most foolish act for advancement.

<a id="p10"></a>Now, people do not usually enter preparation with the idea of serving anything but personal interests. They want more pleasure and less pain. At least, that is the avowed goal. Genuine education seems to be confusing here because it provides a challenge and asks you to apply yourself to the situations where you are needed the most, yet which may also be the greatest areas of avoidance. Then you feel more pain. "I thought this education was to escape pain, to become a happy person, to finally answer the most important questions."

<a id="p11"></a>When I say, therefore, that your education is not for you, it is very important that this must be a tacit understanding. Obviously it requires opening the whole question of "What is my education for? If not for me, what is it for? Am I merely to be sacrificed for another will, even if it is greater than my own?" In truth, your education is for you and not for you both, for you need to discern a Greater Reality in life, give yourself to it and participate through all the stages. That is your greatest opportunity. Therefore, we aim to take people to the greatest pleasure, not little pleasures to merely ameliorate their sense of loneliness but great pleasure where loneliness is never thought of.

<a id="p12"></a>When you leave this world, you will return to your Spiritual Family, and all this will become so obvious. Your memory will return to you, and there will be no question. But while you are here this memory seems buried. It does not seem relevant to the world you see.

<a id="p13"></a>People who are not beset by personal tragedy are in a position to contribute a great deal to the world. Their contribution is their reward and produces satisfaction, purpose, meaning and direction not only for them but for everyone who can perceive them.

<a id="p14"></a>What did the great teachers in this world do? Was their contribution from doing? Is there anything tangible left behind but recorded words? Yet their impact is greater than standing armies and natural disasters. You are still feeling the reverberation of their contribution. That is how great their contribution was. That is how great their satisfaction was. You were created to give because you are an extension of God. Many of you do not like the idea of God, but that is all right because you are religious people. You may substitute your own words for mine if you find them too difficult.

<a id="p15"></a>Therefore, your education is not for you. It is for the world. The greatest satisfaction a human being can experience is being a vehicle for something greater that loves the world. All individuals who have contributed greatly to the world in any capacity in any arena-by thought, word, action or deed-always felt a Greater Power behind them. Your greatness is to give greatness which you yourself have not created for personal aims. People become engaged in the study of Knowledge and they often say, "I don't know why I am studying this." As they advance, they realize their desire for personal power, personal control, management of their lives and personal protection from pain or adversity is perhaps not being fully satisfied, but something else is happening in its place that seems far more substantial, sound and reliable.

<a id="p16"></a>People want us to make their lives easy, and we want to make their lives genuine, so sometimes there is a misunderstanding here. When your life is genuine and you are doing something you believe to be truly important, then you have a foundation for confidence and self-worth. Then you realize that your ability to communicate with others and the need to maintain a healthy body and mind are simply necessary. When people do not do what they know they must do for their well-being, it is because they do not yet value their own life. You cannot simply create value by telling yourself you are worth it. Value comes because your life is for something, perhaps beyond definition, but for something nonetheless.

<a id="p17"></a>When people come to see us, they are often very eager to have information about their future. That is all they want. "How will all this work out? Will I win?" Sometimes they are disappointed because we give education. What is the value of an answer if it cannot be fully embodied? Each of you already possesses enough answers to enlighten a thousand people, but you do not have the desire or capacity yet to fully embody them.

<a id="p18"></a>When we talk of education, we have to talk of practice because practice is what people do. It is the practical form of their devotion or adherence to their pursuit. People say, "I want to know my purpose," and I say, "Do this. When you do this, you will find out." Now, practice takes a long time. It is not quick and easy and its rewards come in their own way. People do many foolish things with practice, but you cannot judge a teaching by the behavior of the students. How can the students' behavior be exemplary if they are being honest?

<a id="p19"></a>The greater the teaching, the worse the abuse will be because fewer will be able to use it and progress. The more simple, basic and short-lived the education, the more people can exemplify it, but over time it will not meet their overall need. The education that I am speaking of is not for everyone because not everyone is ready for it. In fact, everyone does not need to be ready for it, only certain individuals. If you are not satisfied with the normal range of things, this may qualify you. If you are looking for something greater, this may indicate that you may be requiring a greater education which means a greater responsibility.

<a id="p20"></a>When people speak of religion and argue about which is best or truest or has God's blessing to the greatest degree, we must always ask, "What do you do for practice? What are you doing that is advancing yourself and enabling you to be exemplary to others?" That is the only question we need to ask about religion. God is not interested in religion, only in advancement. That is all that matters. If you think of it like this, it will make sense to you. You can leave all sectarian controversies behind for those who cannot practice. It is like learning anything, is it not? To learn a musical instrument or a physical talent, you must practice. It is just like that. But practice must be specific and have guidance from someone who is competent. It also has a mysterious aspect which engages aspects of you which are really beyond understanding. It is appropriate that education have a mysterious aspect that cannot be logically approached.

<a id="p21"></a>This season you celebrate Jesus. Jesus is still having impact because his education has a resonating effect throughout the world. It is a call to education actually. When he says, "Come follow me," which is the essence of his Teaching, he is saying, "Engage in the form of education in which I am engaged." But Christianity as religion does not teach education. It teaches the necessity of belief and the fundamentals of moral behavior. Rarely does it actually engage people in fundamental education, particularly beyond the realm of simple personal behavior. Its errors are manifest, but that is not a reason to discount it as a form of education.

<a id="p22"></a>Why are you being educated? You do not really know. Then why do it? Because you must. Understanding the purpose of education is based completely on your advancement. Let us give this idea for you to consider. People who are not satisfied with the normal range of things ask very great questions that perhaps other people find foolish or unnecessary, such as "What is the meaning of my life? Is there life beyond death of the body? What is the purpose of my life, if it has a purpose at all?" These things can only be known in hindsight. At the end of your life, you'll look back and see what has transpired. Then you'll have a much more substantial view of your life's purpose than someone who is looking into the unknown future and trying to speculate.

<a id="p23"></a>What is important about purpose is that it is something you wake up with in the morning and go to bed with at night. It is not a definition that you attempt to live by. It is a living experience in your life-perhaps fleeting, not every moment, but consistent enough that it provides consistency itself. Then you realize that truth does not fluctuate. You do.

<a id="p24"></a>That is why we tell people, "Follow Knowledge once you can learn to identify it within yourself." Very simple. Not easy to do. It means that you have more power than answers. It is a very vital form of education in all the ways that it manifests itself to you. Regarding great questions about life, these things are seen in hindsight and rarely understood in the moment. You say, "What am I doing now? Why am I doing this?" Look back. Without regret or disappointment, your past illustrates preparation for education.

<a id="p25"></a>In a previous discourse, we spoke of education as having three levels. The first is education for physical survival, which is development of the body. The second is education of the personality for social survival, which is the development of personal behavior and forms of communication. Ninety-nine percent of your education that you can recognize has been for these two purposes. So, I wish to congratulate each one of you for having succeeded! Your body has survived and you are able, to some degree, to function in society. Very good!

<a id="p26"></a>The third arena of education is the development of Knowledge, education in a whole other category. People think that education in this category is completely mysterious and cannot be described, that it is all by chance-meaning that you can be passive and maybe some day God can do something with you, hopefully tomorrow, not today, and that if you sit back, God will kick you in the pants or give you some great avenue to travel. But in actuality, education in the realm of Knowledge, though it has a mysterious aspect and a mysterious destiny, is as fundamental in its daily application as learning to tie shoes or read books. It requires that you develop stillness and concentration of mind, discernment in relationship, sensitivity to communication, patience, perseverance, self application, trust, and tolerance-fundamental things that are actually part of your learning in the other two categories.

<a id="p27"></a>In terms of practice, Knowledge is not so mysterious and can be quantified and applied. Therefore, do not confuse the Mystery with the application. If I give a practice to someone who is a student, I can speak of the practice. Very simple: Do this and this once a day or twice or once a week or whatever. Nothing mysterious. What is mysterious is that you do not understand how it works. You do not need to understand how it works, unless you have mastered it and are preparing to teach it to others. When you are learning to tie your shoes, it does not matter to you how the educational process works. It simply matters that you accomplish the task. Then, if necessary, you can study the methodology of learning this so you can teach it to another.

<a id="p28"></a>We teach in the third arena of education, but the third arena of education completes the first two. This means as you begin to study Knowledge and experience it as an active living force in your life, it provides information, direction and discernment for you. In other words, it provides inner guidance. You realize that you will have to further develop your physical vehicle. You will also have to further develop your personality-who you think you are.

<a id="p29"></a>Knowledge will take you beyond these two arenas, but it will require their sound development as well. You cannot escape worldly problems and have any hope of true advancement in the realm of Knowledge. It will always bring you back to exactly where you need to be. Those who engage with it with a desire to avoid the irresolutions that have plagued them thus far will find little relief here, for there is little relief without the completion of past and present irresolutions. That enables you to go into new territory because the past has been completed. Education is always for advancement. Advancement is always to take you beyond where you are today, and this is religion.

<a id="p30"></a>Now I will tell you something of myself and where I have come from. Perhaps I can use my life to illustrate the points I have made so far. I am no longer living in the physical, but in my physical life I was from a race called Arranon, a very small society which in actuality demonstrated the possibility of mass education-both in development of the physical vehicle, the personality and Knowledge. Much farther had they gone in these three realms than humankind has gone and their accomplishments, successes and failures are well worth documenting. My people discovered some very fundamental aspects of living in the physical. Certain of them took this education very far and applied it to the actual management of society. Education was the principle commodity and product, and it enabled them to produce technology at a remarkable level.

<a id="p31"></a>Do not look to the world and say, "Give me purpose, meaning and direction." It is you who must give this to the world because the world is asking this of you. The world is asking you, "Give me purpose, meaning and direction." There is a great gulf between these two approaches. You are not the source of truth, but you are possibly a vehicle for its full expression, which will elevate you so far beyond your current self-evaluations that it will almost look like you are Godlike. You will be Godlike, but God is still greater. If you are truly a vehicle for something greater, it will express itself. You will be required to both acknowledge your advancement and to also maintain a very healthy form of humility because you will always seem small compared to the greatness that is at hand. Though you may seem greater than you were in the past, something will always be overshadowing you, and from this you will derive your inspiration and your unique individual contribution to life.

<a id="p32"></a>Everyone has personal needs. Everyone has greater possibilities. When you truly entertain a greater possibility, your personal needs will be satisfied. If we entertain only your personal needs, nothing will be satisfied-not even your personal needs. I have mentioned the accomplishments of my people in terms of technology because that is part of what your world needs, but technology is only the result of something greater happening.

<a id="p33"></a>There are two forms of learning that are predominant in any one person: They will learn through ideas or through feeling, predominantly one over the other. Therefore, the curriculum must be unique and have specific applications to different types of people with different orientations. Anyone who is advanced as a teacher will learn this and will be flexible enough to administer it. Yet because the education is not for you, it has a uniformity that does not always conform itself to the personal preferences and idiosyncrasies of the individuals involved, and that is good as well.

<a id="p34"></a>The first thing that is really important to learn, which seems so simple that people do not even think of it, is how to learn. People think they already know how to learn. You remember the idea-study for a test, pass the exam, unless, of course, it is something that is very important which you will be using for the rest of your life. If that can be identified, you will want to learn it in a whole different manner.

<a id="p35"></a>So, one of our first challenges in education is for people to learn how to be students. It is required for a student to have an open mind and to assume the responsibility of practice. This requires a very honest reckoning regarding your current skills. In other words, you must find out what your real starting point is, not the starting point that you wish for or hope for. Many people want to live as if they are living the life they want and not the life they have. Very few people are genuinely aware of their strengths and weaknesses at any given moment, so these things must be discovered to a great degree. That is part of the education.

<a id="p36"></a>It is absolutely necessary to teach the education once you have progressed in it, and the first thing a teacher must learn is how to discern genuine interest and ability in students. The second thing the teacher must learn is how to be flexible in the teaching in order to reach people. The third thing is how to be uniform in the teaching so that all people can be aimed in the proper direction.

<a id="p37"></a>Therefore, the first goal of education is learning to be a student. Then the advancement can happen very quickly. Then you must learn each step. Each step is very, very clear. What you don't understand is why it works or where it came from, but it is clear because it is practical and you can, in time, see its direct relevance to your situation.

<a id="p38"></a>Because you are religious people, you have come here for education. Education is to satisfy your personal need for purpose, meaning and direction. Everyone must begin learning to be a student, which means having an open mind, not claiming things that are not established and not denying things that are present. The student must accept the responsibility of training in a curriculum that they did not create for themselves. In this way they are able to go where they could never take themselves.

<a id="p39"></a>Our curriculum deals with the development of Knowledge, which begins with your ability to have profound insight in your current situations, beyond your personal preferences or fears. It also develops your ability to act upon this in an effective manner. That is the form of education that we are here to represent, though it has been presented throughout human history, and indeed throughout the history of all races. Your advantage here is that it is presented without the burden of human superstition, so it does not have the residue of the past to make access to it more difficult.

<a id="p40"></a>Human superstition is the belief in something that is not there. If you think of your religious customs, consider how difficult it is to get to the education. What is the education? What is the practice? Superstition is when people attempt to use religion to gain power over one another, to ameliorate their own sense of guilt or blame and to satisfy personal ends. Training seems to be very difficult, so people wish to have ideas only and they will accept whatever is most gratifying.

<a id="p41"></a>One of our first jobs in working with people is to bring them back to unknowing so they can know something directly. People say, "I thought I was going to know more as a result of being with you and now I know less!" And I say that is very good. At least now what you know is something worth knowing, and you are unfettered with knowing things that have no value. Superstition is the result of making conclusions without understanding. It permeates all of human thought. When people first encounter religion, that is often what they encounter-the superstition alone. So, we have given you the means to approach religion by asking, "What is its practice?" not "Is this truth?" Truth is beyond religion. Religion is an approach to truth. The approach is practice. Therefore, what is the practice?

<a id="p42"></a>When you try to believe things that are beyond your experience, you run into problems. It is better simply to practice something that is useful and learn to become a student and practitioner. Do you want to find out what you know within yourself? I ask you. That is the starting point of training in Knowledge. Approaching Knowledge is like climbing a great mountain. On the way up the mountain you find beautiful places to rest and find comfort. You lie down and sleep for a few thousand years. "This is it! More beautiful than I ever thought! More wondrous! I can see! Now I can see the valleys below. I have true perspective. It is so beautiful here. I shall reside here." But the student must press on.

<a id="p43"></a>You must at some point say, "What is it all for? Why am I doing this? Why not just find a nice place to live, get married and have a simple life? Call it a day! Why not do that? So simple. Other people are doing it. They are happy, somewhat. Why do I need more?" Take the next step. The curriculum is mysterious because you do not know where it comes from or how it works, but it is not intangible in what it asks of you. I say practice. Keep practicing. You may say, "But there are so many other things to do." I say, "Keep practicing." It will take you there. The practice is sufficient to take you there. Go with that. The way is not difficult. It is that people want other things that makes it seem confusing.

<a id="p44"></a>Our curriculum is very simple to do and very hard to understand because understanding is not necessary. Understanding comes naturally when you are progressing. Forget about understanding. Do the practice. Involve yourself in education. Like learning a musical instrument, one day you are able to play. But until then, you are working on it. Who knows when that day will be when you can sit down and play the piece?

<a id="p45"></a>I should like to make this point about practice: If you are to engage in a practice, you should at least give it a year. If you begin and you find it is a practice that is good for you and you are making progress, give it at least a year. If you quit too soon, you will not find out. People are often getting new practices. They get tired of this one, they go on to that one. Tired of that one, they go on to the next one. They never get very far. A practice is like a relationship; it is something you give yourself to. The practice does not wave a magic wand over your head and say, "Now you are wonderful!" You have a relationship with practice. Sometimes practice is very boring. Particularly after the initial excitement wears down, it can become very monotonous. That is when the real work begins. If it is the right practice for you, it will take you very far because you will have a greater relationship with it. Practice is like marriage. Do you throw a marriage away because things are becoming dull or difficult? It ceased to be a thrilling love affair; now it is all work! Do you say, "Marriage is over. Let us find a new marriage." No, this is something that requires devotion and persistence.

<a id="p46"></a>The practices that we give are very simple to do. They require very little time but must be done consistently, regardless of what you think of them. They will work, regardless of whether you like them or not, if you keep doing them. They train the mind so that the mind can become a vehicle for something greater. Training can be monotonous. People do not train; they stimulate themselves. Anything that is important or that was made required skill and training. Someone didn't wake up one day and build a piano. They trained to do it.

<a id="p47"></a>Do you want to have the experience of Knowledge? Do you want to see God in everything? You must train. Then you can be grateful to God. "Thank you for giving me this." Then you know God is there because God has given you something to work with. Not mysterious. Everyday you get up and do this. Not mysterious. You just do it! It is important that true practice be somewhat not understandable. If it is understandable, then it does not take you any further.

<a id="p48"></a>One of the most simple practices is this: You sit quietly and repeat the word "Rahn." Repeat that. "Rahn." That practice is enough to elevate your mind to a phenomenal level, but to get to the phenomenal level, you will go through all your attitudes, feelings and beliefs about the practice. One day it will thrill you. Next day it will bore you. It will take you through everything that you are already going through. If you do it, it will provide consistency for you. It will empower your mind over time. Not everyone can do that practice. Some people are so mentally active, they cannot sit still for anything, so they have to do something. Other people are more contemplative or more introverted. That practice is easier for them. Anything you do that engages your mind with something greater that is done consistently will yield a result you could not produce for yourself.

<a id="p49"></a>Why do people get involved in religion? Because they are looking for a result they realize they cannot produce for themselves. God becomes real when you need God. When you realize you need God everyday and not just when things are bad, well, then you begin to acknowledge a greater relationship. If you want to see God in everything, then you must see everything as it really is and feel God while you are seeing everything as it really is. Otherwise, you will want to turn everything into a beautiful experience and you become ridiculously dishonest about physical life. If you look at a table and feel God, it's still a table, nothing magical about the table, but you feel something greater while you are experiencing this, it has nothing to do with the table. God is like an invisible substance that permeates everything. It does not make things look better. That is why when you are experiencing God like this, which we call "practicing the Presence," you are not disavowing physical reality, you are simply experiencing something else while you are watching it.

<a id="p50"></a>We teach practices such as gazing to help people develop this sensitivity while they are essentially engaged with something physical. But do you know what the greatest practice of all is and the hardest to do? It is to follow what you know you must do, regardless. To do that you need a powerful mind. You need a strong body. You need to establish confidence within yourself and to learn something about the possible mistakes that you can make. This all requires training and preparation.

<a id="p51"></a>Saying the word "Rahn" is very good if you have a very active mind. "Rahn" gives you something to think about. When you practice using a mantra like this, it brings up everything your mind is already engaged in. You keep coming back to the practice-back, back, back-because the practice is more important than your thoughts or feelings or attitudes. It is like the practice is your God for awhile. It is the closest part of God to you, your practice. That is why we speak of religion in terms of practice. After all, God is the end. We are talking of the means. The means are what is important in religion, not the end. You will only know God when you get there, but you must do something to get there.
